QUESTION 2 — REFLECTION
Which part of the proposal is most persuasive for a leadership audience, and why?

The ROI projection is the most persuasive section, because it converts a vague operational
complaint into an auditable figure — 60 OOM events x 38 minutes = 38 hours per month — that
any executive can recalculate, challenge, and hold me to after implementation. Its credibility
comes largely from arguing against itself: charging 3.4 hours of human oversight back against
the savings and conceding that 15 percent of incidents still require a person signals honest
estimation rather than advocacy, which is precisely what makes the remaining 42.9 hours
believable to an audience trained to discount optimistic engineering forecasts. The supporting
detail that earns the most trust is the circuit breaker escalating to PagerDuty instead of
silently patching, since it answers leadership's real unspoken concern — that automation will
conceal defects rather than resolve them.
